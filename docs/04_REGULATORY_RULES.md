
# Regulatory and Tax Rules

Status: Research notes and release checklist. Not legal advice.

## 1. Authoritative Sources

Use official sources as the primary reference:

- Reserve Bank of India: https://www.rbi.org.in/
- CBIC: https://cbic-gst.gov.in/
- GST Council: https://gstcouncil.gov.in/

Issuer-specific information must be checked against the cardholder agreement, Most Important Terms and Conditions (MITC), and official fee schedule.

## 2. RBI Requirements to Research

Verify the latest applicable requirements concerning:

- Minimum Amount Due disclosures.
- Finance-charge calculation methodology.
- Past-due account treatment.
- Three-day period.
- Late-payment and penal charges.
- Credit information company reporting.
- Effective dates of regulatory amendments.

## 3. Three-Day Period

Do not describe this as three free days.

The applicable RBI provision must be checked for its exact treatment of past-due accounts, penal charges and credit reporting.

Suggested cautious wording:

"RBI requirements provide a three-day period for the specified past-due treatment. Whether a late-payment charge applies, and how much it is, depends on applicable rules and your card issuer's terms. This simulation assumes the payment remains unpaid beyond the relevant period."

### Reviewed RBI provisions

The RBI Master Direction - Credit Card and Debit Card - Issuance and Conduct Directions, 2022, updated on 7 March 2024, was reviewed on 15 November 2026. Paragraph 9(b)(v) permits past-due reporting or penal charges only after the account has remained past due for more than three days; the count is computed from the statement due date, and late fees apply to the post-due outstanding, not the total amount due. Paragraph 9(b)(vi) limits interest to outstanding amounts adjusted for payments, refunds and reversals. Paragraph 9(b)(ii) prohibits capitalizing unpaid taxes, levies and charges. The Direction requires issuers to disclose their interest and MAD methodology; it does not prescribe one universal MAD percentage.

Primary source: https://www.rbi.org.in/Scripts/BS_ViewMasDirections.aspx?id=12300 (updated 7 March 2024; reviewed 15 November 2026).

Issuer example reviewed: https://www.sbicard.com/en/most-important-terms-and-conditions.page. The published SBI Card material gives an issuer-specific MAD/payment-allocation example; it is not a rule for other issuers.

## 4. Late Payment Fee

₹750 is an illustrative assumption.

It is not a universal RBI-prescribed fee.

Actual charges may depend on:

- Issuer.
- Card product.
- Outstanding amount.
- Applicable fee slab.
- Regulatory requirements.

## 5. GST

Verify current GST applicability, rate and taxable value.

Keep GST separately itemized.

Do not assume every charge has the same tax treatment without verification.

The app's 18% rate is a disclosed scenario assumption from the supplied golden fixture only. No universal legal claim about GST on interest or late fees is made. CBIC/GST Council primary-source verification of current rates, exemptions, and taxable bases remains mandatory before public release.

## 6. Regulatory Changes

Regulatory amendments may have future effective dates.

Check the current RBI requirements and amendments effective from 1 April 2027 before release.

Do not hardcode future regulatory provisions as if they are already effective.

## 7. Release Requirements

Before public release:

- Verify all claims against primary sources.
- Record source URLs.
- Record publication dates.
- Record effective dates.
- Document the rule implemented.
- Obtain appropriate compliance review.

Never invent regulatory rules or claim legal certainty.
